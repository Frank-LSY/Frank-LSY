I am a Ph.D. student in Biomedical Informatics at UTHealth Houston and a member of the TEAM-AI group, advised by Hongfang Liu. My research focuses on developing intelligent and reliable AI systems for biomedical and clinical applications, spanning large language model reasoning, multimodal learning, longitudinal representation learning, and clinical decision support.

I am particularly interested in how AI can acquire, integrate, and reason over heterogeneous biomedical evidence to understand patient trajectories, improve medication safety, and support personalized healthcare. My current work includes dynamic postoperative risk prediction and longitudinal analyses of treatment and adverse-event trajectories. Alongside this work, I explore how interpretable models and multi-agent systems could support patient-specific clinical reasoning.

Background & research philosophy

Growing up in a family of physicians, I often heard my parents discuss clinical practice and medical decision-making. Those conversations made me curious about the gap between the wealth of available clinical data and the knowledge clinicians can use in an individual patient's care. That question led me from computer science to biomedical informatics.

After earning my bachelor's degree in computer science, I completed an M.S. in Biomedical Informatics at the University of Pittsburgh, where I began studying predictive models for heart failure. I then returned to China to work on data-driven chronic disease management and software development. These experiences taught me to value systems that combine methodological rigor with a clear clinical purpose.

At UTHealth Houston, discussions with Dr. Liu, Xiaoyang Ruan, Liwei Wang, and colleagues at McGovern Medical School helped sharpen my interest in perioperative care. Dr. Liu's advice to start with the clinical problem and design the right tool has become central to my research: the question should guide the method, and the method should fit the clinical workflow.

Looking ahead, I aim to develop explainable, patient-specific decision support for perioperative risk management, including orthopedic care. One direction is a multi-agent framework that integrates heterogeneous clinical data and the perspectives of different clinical roles. This remains a research goal, with clinical validation and workflow integration essential to its development. Ultimately, I want my work to help clinicians turn complex evidence into informed, individualized care.

STREAM: Dynamic postoperative AKI prediction

An event-stream Transformer that estimates postoperative acute kidney injury (AKI) risk at surgery and updates it as new clinical events arrive.

→
Event-stream TransformerIrregular, time-stamped EHR

The repository includes OMOP-based cohort preparation, model training, baseline comparisons, calibration, and aggregate analyses. Patient-level data are not shared.

Repository-reported performance means across five fold-specific models; dynamic metrics average 120 six-hour windows over 30 days. Retrospective evaluation, not evidence of clinical deployment.

GLP-1 Evidence Atlas: Temporal drug safety

A global-to-local pharmacovigilance study connecting FAERS reporting signals with longitudinal EHR phenotypes and treatment episodes to examine duration-aware patterns in GLP-1/GIP medication safety.

Clinical representationsSemantic mapping & exposure phenotyping

The interactive project page traces signal detection, clinical concept mapping, and treatment-episode reconstruction, with evidence explorers and interpretation of the study's findings. First-author research accepted at the AMIA Annual Symposium 2026.
